What follows:
- "Many sources, clusters, LLM summary, links" is a commodity in 2026: free, automated, often with
  more sources (PulseAugur) or with community signal we do not collect (AINews: Reddit/X/Discord;
  the owner rules out paid social APIs).
- Not found anywhere: a mechanical check of every AI sentence against the cited article text
  (`parse_brief`: exact quote, known ids, numbers and names in the cited text) and extraction that
  keeps only names and quotes present in the article. That is our one clear differentiator. The
  reader sees it only as "Written by AI from the linked articles; check the sources." Limit: the
  check is about presence, not meaning (reversed roles or a dropped negation pass); R3 now measures
  how often that happens. The positioning below should not claim more than "checked against".
- Second differentiator: a memory of our own corpus. New = first seen by us and announced as new;
  spikes, rising and cooling against our own history; deterministic week and month comparisons.
  Newsletters do not keep such a layer. It is still young (20 days of snapshots, 2 weeks, 0 months)
  and its precision is unmeasured (R3).
- Where we are weaker: distribution (zero), no push channel, a digest of 60-80 items on weekdays
  (competitors send 5-10), no brand or domain.

## Recommendation

**Primary reader: A, the owner's personal AI radar** (later a small circle the owner shares it with).
Runner-up: C, analysts and managers on weekly and monthly signals, the direction to grow into if
the owner wants an audience, once history and trend quality are proven.

Why A: it is the only reader the site reaches today; it fits the costs (paid calls capped per day
for one reader); it explains decisions already taken (B4: recall over precision, a wrong New item
costs one reader a few seconds). B is a distribution business against free incumbents; C needs
history we will only have in early 2027; D has no consumer.

Next 2-3 things for A:
1. **B5 Telegram, as a private channel**: the daily brief + New + link to `/` after publication,
   the weekly brief on Mondays. Removes "remember to open the site" and gives the first usage
   number. Telegram Bot API is free; no new provider calls. Channel choice is a product question
   already listed in B5/B10.
2. **Page-view counter** (Measurement 1).
3. **B6 watchlist**: alert when a chosen company, model or topic appears in New, Top stories or a
   spike; reuses entities, $0 provider cost. Delivered through the same channel.

## Positioning (for the owner to accept or reject)

A personal AI-news radar. Every night it reads about ninety lab, company, research and media
sources and makes one page: a short brief in which every sentence is checked against the article
it cites, what was announced for the first time, which stories several publishers carried, and
how this week compares with the last. No ads, no hype, every claim one click from its source.

## Owner decisions (2026-10-31, after the report)

The discussion with the owner went past the first recommendation (the owner as reader):
- Bare facts without an author's opinion are of little use to a passive human reader; no reader
  media (Telegram for readers, entity pages, RAG chat, video are frozen).
- Primary consumer: AI agents and pipelines; the niche is an **AI release radar**: what labs and
  vendors announced for the first time, each fact with a quote from the source. General news for
  agents is taken by funded players with free tiers (NewsCatcher NewsMCP, AskNews); neither marks
  first-seen nor checks facts against the text (from their public pages, 2026-10-31).
- No direct monetization; the return is skill and portfolio with numbers. Consulting, if any, is
  the owner's own time (free is fine).
- Attention order: (1) development by agents with the owner's minutes, cost and defects per
  feature measured; (2) quality evaluation (R3); (3) the release radar for agents.
- Radar order: R1 and R3 first; request counting by client type (no IP); a reference set for
  completeness and detection delay; MCP server and feed, listed in the MCP registry; report after
  4-6 weeks. Achievement metrics: completeness, precision, delay against a competitor, share of
  facts with a source quote; usage (external agents per week, returning clients, calls per day)
  is a bonus.

### Owner decisions, part 2: the Herald (2026-10-31)

- Yes: a public satirical outlet built on the real daily and weekly signals: "the first media
  outlet of an AI-run world". The facts stay real and sourced; only the framing is fiction. AI's
  influence is real today (answers, feeds, code; this project is built and run by agents), so the
  satire works because its core is true.
- English first; Telegram first (free, automatic); Instagram after a one-time setup by the owner;
  X only after its API price is checked (money question).
- Rejected by the owner: models as citizens (humans would be second class); "two peoples" (humans
  and models as equal citizens). Open: the form of the AI world. The owner wants several concepts
  that need not look like human states ("AI is not human"); a "Gosplan" (AI as central planner)
  is one candidate, not a choice. Design goes to a separate session:
  `docs/work/prompt-herald-world-concepts-2026-10-31.md`.
- Kept from the discussion: no Soviet or other real state symbols (some countries restrict them);
  fiction never puts words into real people's mouths and never implies endorsement; vendors'
  claims stay attributed; the owner is the Founder (the honorary pension, a lifetime compute quota,
  goes into the world bible); a monthly "editorial chronicle" with real numbers about how agents run
  the project.
